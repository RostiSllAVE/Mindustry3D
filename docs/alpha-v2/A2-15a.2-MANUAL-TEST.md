1. **Start with a broad sandy coast, then a rocky coast.** Pick maps with a substantial water edge, not only a small inland puddle. Fly low near the boundary and look along the shore, rotate through a corner, then rise to an oblique view and a very high overview. Water should remain a broad surface; islands should be sparse dry land, never blue mountains. Sand should meet a gentle shallow shelf, rock should meet a steeper shore. Compare near shallow water with darker distant ocean. A small short edge pool should close as a basin rather than become an infinite water wedge. Check for cracks, diagonal streaks, stretched cliff patterns and water laid over dry ridges.
2. **Check material identity and horizon details on several maps.** Try a dense green/forest map, a mixed stone/sand/water/snow map, and hotrock/magmarock/slag. Mostly sand should stay sand, green should stay green, and narrow transition bands should not wash whole regions into mud. Look for retained far ridge/dune/forested silhouettes with haze behind them. Islands/outcrops/structural traces are optional and map-dependent, not guaranteed everywhere. Industrial traces should appear only rarely on strongly artificial maps; natural mountain walls alone must not produce factories or structural motifs. Repeat at night. On a water-bearing map, use the existing permitted FPV immersion to inspect the dimmer/darker far bottom; native immersion tint/droplets must still behave normally on exiting.
3. **Return to normal play and test lifecycle/performance.** Turn the automatic background OFF/ON, use F5 and Tab, place a block, pause/resume with F3, change blueprint priority and try ordinary 3D selection/copy-paste. Save/reload and switch maps or campaign sectors. Scenery should reproduce consistently for an unchanged map, release the previous map's geometry and never expand physical/buildable boundaries. Compare low and high views for frame stalls on your hardware. A one-time initial atlas/cache entry hitch is distinct from continuous per-frame slowdown. On Linux repeat the coast, night and immersion checks; multiplayer testing, if available, requires this exact same final JAR on every peer.
