# A2-15a.2 — Readable coasts, ocean depth and automatic distant features

Baseline: owner-tested A2-15a.1 `7379648`, on the existing `main` branch. Local diagnostic only; no public release version change, push or Workshop update. This extends the automatic background, not manual presets or a new world-generation/physics system.

**Final evidence: PASS within the automated/scripted scope below.** Java17 and stock Steam25 each pass 151/151 regression programs. Both comparison builds complete 2,592 actual host-rendered frames with zero GL errors. Physical owner acceptance remains pending.

## Confirmed causes

1. Water was a blended influence on the same height field as land. Mixed ocean/mountain weights could produce raised geometry, while a separately evaluated, warped shader profile could label that geometry as water. Colour and height could therefore disagree: a valid-looking hill was shaded blue, or an apparent water boundary crossed a different geometric boundary. Neither a global texture filter nor smaller waves could fix that mismatch.
2. Legacy section-centre interpolation blended colours and weights throughout the space between neighbouring sections. Minority materials also contributed to section-average palettes. Even tiled detail could then look like a muddy material wash, with wide diagonal biome fans.
3. The old distant land-height envelope diminished toward the far limit while atmospheric fade increased. Haze replaced large-scale closure rather than sitting behind an identifiable ridge or ocean horizon. The coastline had no shared open-ocean versus short-basin policy or explicit seabed/surface separation.
4. Terrain detail used world X/Z projection even on steep geological closing faces. Horizontal density remained correct, but a nearly vertical face could still smear its texture vertically. The host atlas itself did not need its global wrap or filtering changed.

## Shared landscape and biome rules

`BorderBiomeProfile` retains its bounded strips and existing natural/artificial content policy. A separate readable material core now selects the dominant family and averages only that family's source palette. Majority natural walls are land; a coherent open-water majority with only sparse natural rock remains sea. Trees remain a separate supporting-ground/canopy identity; industrial walls are not geological mountains.

`sampleReadable` keeps each section mostly unmixed, with approximately **10% of the smaller adjacent section width** reserved for a centred transition. For a usual eight-tile section that is about 0.8 tiles in total. Continuous cyclic corner blending remains, but neighbouring sand, rock, snow and green ground no longer wash across entire sections. The old broad sampling method remains available to existing consumers.

`HorizonLandscape` is the single derived policy for land/water identity, material, ground height, depth and coastline style. Geometry consumes these same results directly. The former separately warped fragment-profile mask is removed; a water triangle cannot independently acquire mountain height from a different biome lookup. A restrained deterministic spatial warp bends distant zones without moving native map cells.

Contiguous water-run length distinguishes broad sea edges from small enclosed water runs. A run is open sea when it reaches the bounded map-relative threshold (at least `max(32, 0.60 × min(map width, map height))` tiles, or 28% of the perimeter). Short runs close into decorative basins farther out. This is an edge-derived heuristic, not hydrological analysis of neighbouring sectors. Both sides of a narrow shore use the same water-run identity.

## Ocean, seabed and coast geometry

Water areas now have two geometric layers in the cached background:

- A seabed which deepens with outward distance. Open ocean approaches a 16-tile visual depth; small basins approach four tiles. These are decorative heights outside the native map only.
- A water surface fixed at the existing liquid visual level, with small slow shader swells and ripples. Terrain mountains never displace this plane. Coast-crossing triangles are explicitly clipped at the shared water/land boundary instead of covering a dry hill with a blue material mask.

The four-tile collar uses the real native boundary heights and water level. Its water portion is clipped to the same coast sign. The collar and first ring share the union of native integer and fractional biome knots at distances four and 12; regression assertions inspect actual submitted vertices on both joins. Native edge heights interpolate across split faces. Waves are pinned at zero depth and the quiet 12-tile join. Companion trees independently re-check their own shore and forest eligibility. Sand coasts use a gentle shelf/beach ramp; rocky coasts use a shorter, steeper transition; forest/green and icy coasts use their own supporting-ground palette and form. Narrow foam-like approaches and wet/deep colour progression improve the shoreline without changing native liquid rendering, exposure, buoyancy or collision.

The final graphical inspection also confirmed false rectangular foam: the technical map apron supplied a constant shore value to the foam function. That apron now explicitly excludes foam; actual clipped coastlines still provide their real coast value. This removes that artificial border contribution, without claiming all native/background palette differences are invisible.

Ocean depth darkens the surface toward a deeper blue instead of lifting it into terrain. When the actual rendered camera is below the liquid level, distant below-water background geometry is increasingly attenuated; the far bottom is less readable. This is a background shader effect. Existing in-map immersion tint and exit droplets remain separate and are not validated by merely placing the scripted gallery camera underwater.

## Stronger horizon and bounded secondary details

Far land retains biome-aware raised closure: mountain and volcanic ridges, rocky walls, forested hills, snow ridges or low desert ridges/dunes. Haze starts behind those masses rather than flattening them away. Open water keeps a broad water horizon. This is low-poly inferred scenery, not a promise of a physically traversable neighbouring world.

`HorizonComposition` adds a **maximum 4,096-cell**, endpoint-inclusive deterministic sample grid, including map edges. It runs only with horizon-cache rebuilds. It records water, green floor, sand, rock, hot floor, ice, natural walls, plants, decorations and structural/industrial content separately. Its seed uses map dimensions, sampled coordinates, stable content names and flags, never time, session IDs, teams or native RNG.

Optional features are conservative:

- Islands require both a meaningful water fraction and sampled land evidence, plus an actual open-ocean background location. A featureless all-water map does not invent land. Each eight-sided island has a submerged foot, dry rim and small peak using the nearest dry-coast palette, never a blue water hill.
- Rocky outcrops require sampled rock or natural-wall evidence and dry land. They use low-poly angular masses, not extra native blocks.
- Volcanic scars require hot-map evidence and a volcanic local material; they darken sparse patches of that material.
- Structural traces require at least 4% structural/industrial samples and at least three sampled structural cells. Natural mountains and ordinary decorative rocks cannot enable them. Rare open-bottom angular fragments are visual meshes only.
- Existing lightweight forest proxies continue on suitable dry ground with deterministic cluster, scale and silhouette variation. Composition permits green continuation without treating every decoration as a plant.

`HorizonDistantFeatures` caps the additional set at 12 islands, 12 outcrops, six structural fragments and 2,500 vertices in total. Actual gallery counts are lower and source-dependent. Every full footprint is outside the playable rectangle; islands must remain over water, while outcrops/fragments must remain over dry terrain. Grounded feature skirts query the actual submitted terrain triangles. These meshes add no entities, simulation, resources, native collision or per-feature draw calls.

## Texture density and cache ownership

The collar keeps per-face constant atlas rectangles, half-texel insets and repeated world-space coordinates. The private 32×32 native-pattern detail texture keeps repeat wrapping and mip minification; vanilla atlas filtering/wrap remains untouched. Terrain and shallow-water detail stays tied to world units rather than stretching one atlas tile across a distant ring.

Steep slopes and closing faces now blend toward a dominant-axis vertical projection instead of forcing every surface through X/Z. This is a lightweight cliff correction, not a full costly triplanar material system. Colour, wave, depth and atmosphere changes share the same cached geometry inputs. No external assets or new runtime/native dependencies are required.

Cache hits do not rescan map composition or rebuild meshes. World/content/atlas/policy changes and existing border invalidation rebuild derived scenery and dispose old meshes. The private detail texture is retained between worlds and released on atlas replacement/disposal. Interior edits still do not force a background rebuild; composition is a snapshot for decorative context, not a live industrial counter.

## Remaining limitations and acceptance

The generator infers the surroundings from bounded border strips plus a sparse composition snapshot; it does not know actual neighbouring campaign terrain. Small features can be missed by the bounded grid. Open-sea/basin classification, beach/cliff choice and distant forms remain stylized heuristics. Islands do not create navigable land, resources or new playable space; structures are rare decorative fragments. Far vegetation and mountains remain lightweight low-poly forms. Ocean waves are restrained long-distance effects, not a replacement for native in-map liquid animation.

The scripted native submerged view reveals existing in-map bed edge-pinned triangles; they appear in both baseline and final captures and are not changed by this background-only pass. A separate outside-map submerged camera checks the new background attenuation without hiding that limitation.

At very high altitude the rectangle can still be recognized through different native in-map detail, cliffs and material density. Tiled pixel patterns, coarse distant triangles and dominant-axis projection transitions may remain visible in extreme views. There is no promise of invisible boundaries or unique hand-authored scenery on every map. Arbitrary third-party materials/atlases are not visually certified.

Physical Windows owner gameplay/visual acceptance, physical Linux rendering/input/performance and independent multiplayer are **NOT TESTED** in this pass. Existing physics, combat, construction, controls, networking and save formats are out of scope. Use the accompanying manual procedure before acceptance; do not treat a passing scripted scene as a two-computer or physical-control test.
