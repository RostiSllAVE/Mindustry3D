# A2-15a.1 — Horizon materials, geometry and seam polish

Baseline: owner-tested A2-15a `41c03cf091bed55954dede10c525cce9c8c2d6b2`, current `main`. Local diagnostic only; no release metadata bump, push or Workshop update. The exact same diagnostic JAR is required on multiplayer peers.

## Confirmed causes

1. The old collar mapped one atlas tile from the native edge across four world tiles, with corner-dependent oblique faces. Its UV interpolation stretched the source instead of repeating it. The far mesh had **no terrain textures at all**: palette colours and weak procedural modulation could only look smeared/monochromatic. There was no distant material mip chain to fix by changing a global vanilla filter.
2. Six radial rings, coarse section knots and abruptly changing ring-height envelopes produced very large triangles and a recognizable radial shape. Interpolating different biome colours over those triangles created diagonal streaks/material fans, rather than an atlas escaping its region. Native atlas filter/wrap remained unchanged in the baseline and final actual GL probes.
3. Two regular vegetation rows sampled every third section, with a small size range and one diamond-like proxy shape, caused visible repetition. Bilinear placement on nonplanar background quads could also float a proxy above its actual rendered triangles.

## Corrections

`HorizonCollarMesh` is a dedicated cached collar batch. Each face carries a constant atlas rectangle with half-texel insets; the shader repeats **world X/Z coordinates once per vanilla tile**, then remaps them inside that rectangle. It no longer interpolates one stretched UV patch across the whole face. Native atlas filtering/wrapping is untouched. Stock atlas sampling and the bounded rectangle avoid adjacent-region bleed. Arbitrary third-party atlas/filter changes are not certified.

`HorizonDetailTexture` creates one owned32×32 RGBA texture at runtime. Its channels contain normalized luminance patterns from the host's sand, stone, snow and water textures. The native palette supplies colour; the packed patterns supply pixel-art detail at roughly32 texels/world tile. The texture alone uses repeat wrapping, nearest magnification and trilinear mip minification. Hardware mip selection reduces subpixel noise without enlarging texels on distant surfaces. No host textures are shipped in the JAR or read from developer directories. Missing host pixmaps use deterministic procedural detail. Palette averaging is bounded32×32 per shared region instead of the previous8×8 sample.

A separate512×2 owned texture caches continuous border colours and water/sand/ice weights. Fragment shading evaluates the outside-world position and a smooth domain warp to sample this profile; it no longer relies on triangle-interpolated biome colours. Corners wrap cyclically. World-space detail remains continuous across the four-tile collar/far join. Geological closing wedges and the quiet12-tile wave LOD join are preserved. This retains A2-15a classification, mixed regions and existing natural/artificial rules.

`HorizonGeometry` now uses geometric distance bands and restrained world-space multi-scale ridges, dunes and hills, with smooth normals derived from actual cached geometry. There are at least16 knots/side on ordinary small maps; section-based bounds remain on large maps. Nearby detail is denser, distant rings coarser. Small-map mountain amplitude scales with diagonal ×0.28, capped120 tiles; this is outside decorative scenery only. Native mountains/collisions are unchanged. Per-fragment native patterns and restrained lighting give side volume without separate shadow passes or extra FBOs.

Forest candidates have deterministic clustered density, depth, yaw, scale and width variation. A smaller neighbour can form a canopy cluster. Conifers have two open low-poly crowns and trunk strips; other native forms retain their identity. There are at most384 cheap proxies, not thousands of simulated plants. Exact triangle barycentric support keeps their feet on the submitted mesh, rather than a bilinear surface. No per-frame mesh generation or per-tree draw is added.

The architecture still caches per world. Rebuilds release the previous profile texture and both geometry batches. The tiny shared detail texture persists across world changes and is disposed on atlas replacement/renderer disposal. Cache hits do not scan borders or rebuild meshes. Display far range, pilot/picking limits, sky/day/night, physical terrain, construction, liquids, units, networking and save formats are unchanged. No presets or space portal were added.

## Remaining limits and owner acceptance

Textures retain scale rather than smearing. Triangular material streaks and regular forest rows are corrected, but this remains an inferred low-poly background. A repeated source-tile pattern can be recognizable; far forests are clusters/impostors rather than a detailed forest. At very high altitude native in-map detail, artificial/native edge walls and strongly contrasting mixed-biome regions can still reveal the map rectangle. Corner/domain-warp transitions are approximate scenery, not neighbouring sectors. Some geological slopes and native-liquid/distant-wave differences remain visible. Detail uses planar X/Z projection, so near-vertical geological closing faces can still stretch vertically; this is not a full triplanar cliff material system. There is no claim of a perfect invisible border at every angle.

Physical owner visual acceptance, actual Linux rendering/performance, night review and independent two-computer gameplay are NOT TESTED in this pass. Existing input code is byte-identical; scripted GL tests do not replace physical F5/Tab/mouse/menu validation. See A2-15a.1-MANUAL-TEST.md for the three suggested checks. No publication occurred.
